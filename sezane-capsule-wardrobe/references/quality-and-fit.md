## Fabric

Read the composition label or product page, then judge it against what the piece is for.

| Fibre | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Wool, merino | knits, coats, trousers; warm, breathes, resists odour | can pill; hand wash or gentle cycle |
| Alpaca, mohair | soft, airy, warm knits | fluffy fibres shed and pill; delicate |
| Cashmere | very soft knits | thin, cheap cashmere pills fast; quality varies a lot |
| Cotton | shirts, tees, denim, summer knits | thin jersey loses shape; check weight if given |
| Linen | summer shirts, trousers, dresses; cool | wrinkles (part of its charm) |
| Silk | blouses, dresses | delicate, often dry clean, shows water marks |
| Viscose/rayon, lyocell, modal | fluid blouses and dresses, nice drape | can shrink or lose shape if washed hot; lyocell is generally sturdier than viscose |
| Polyester, polyamide, acrylic | small amounts add strength, stretch or shape | high percentages in knits pill and trap heat; less durable feel |
| Elastane | a few % gives comfort in jeans and trousers | high amounts lose recovery and bag out over time |
| Leather, suede | shoes, bags, belts; ages well when cared for | suede needs protection from rain |

Blends are not bad by themselves: a little polyamide can make a fluffy knit last longer. Explain the trade-off rather than judging by "100% natural" alone.

## Construction

Things the user can check in store, on arrival, or ask about:

- Seams straight and even; no loose threads; patterns matched at seams on striped or checked pieces.
- Enough seam allowance and a proper hem, so the piece can be altered.
- Buttons sewn on firmly, buttonholes neatly finished.
- Linings in coats and blazers; good-quality zips.
- Knits: dense rather than see-through when stretched a little.

Remind the user to inspect pieces as soon as they arrive, while returns are still possible, and to check the current return conditions on the site rather than assuming.

## Fit and cut

- Shoulders are the hardest thing to alter, so blazers, coats and shirts should fit at the shoulder. Hems, sleeves and waists can be adjusted by a tailor.
- For trousers and jeans, the rise and hip fit matter most; length is easily altered.
- Read Sézane's fit notes ("fitted", "oversized", "take your usual size") and the size guide for each piece; cuts vary, so the user's usual size may not apply.
- Customer reviews are useful for sizing and how a fabric wears over time.
- A well-cut piece that fits *the user's* body and life beats a "perfect basic" on paper. If a cut repeatedly doesn't work for them, steer away from it, even if it is a classic.

## Second-hand buying

Second-hand is ideal for past-season pieces, for trying a cut at a lower price, and for higher-value items like coats, leather bags and wool knits.

Before buying, check:

- **The exact piece:** the name and season if the listing gives them, so the user can look up the original composition and fit notes.
- **Size and measurements:** ask the seller for flat measurements (pit to pit, length, sleeve, waist, inseam) and compare them with a piece the user owns that fits well. This is the most reliable way to avoid a bad fit.
- **Condition:** photos of the label, the underarms (pilling), the cuffs and collar, the seat and inner thighs on trousers, the corners and handles on bags, and the soles on shoes. Ask about any stains, holes or smells.
- **Price:** compare with the original price and other listings of the same piece; for items in "very good" condition, paying close to the original price only makes sense for something rare and much-wanted.

Questions to send the seller (adapt to the language of the platform):

- "Could you share the flat measurements: armpit to armpit, length and sleeve?"
- "Are there any flaws, pilling or stains not visible in the photos?"
- "Could you add a photo of the composition label?"
