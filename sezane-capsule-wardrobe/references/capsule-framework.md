# Capsule framework

Use this as a starting point and adapt it to the user's life, climate and style. A remote worker in a mild climate needs a very different list from someone in an office in a cold city.

## Palette

Build the palette in three layers:

1. **Base neutrals (3–4):** the colours most bottoms, coats, shoes and bags come in. Common sets:
   - Navy, écru/cream, camel, denim blue (classic French)
   - Black, grey, white, denim (city, high contrast)
   - Chocolate brown, beige, cream, khaki (warm)
2. **Light neutrals for tops:** white, cream, light blue stripes, soft grey.
3. **Accents (1–2):** a colour the user loves and that suits them (e.g. red, burgundy, forest green, soft pink). Use them for knitwear, a blouse, a scarf or a bag.

Rule of thumb: every new piece should go with at least two of the base neutrals.

Metals: pick one hardware tone (gold or silver) for bags, belts and shoes where possible, so accessories go with everything.

## Core pieces by category

Typical core for a four-season, mixed-life wardrobe. Quantities are rough guides, not targets to fill.

| Category | Core pieces | Rough count |
|---|---|---|
| Tops | white/cream shirt or blouse, striped top, plain tees, one more dressy blouse | 6–10 |
| Knitwear | a cardigan, a crew or V-neck jumper, a warmer chunky knit | 3–5 |
| Bottoms | straight or wide well-cut trousers, straight jeans, a midi skirt or a second trouser | 3–5 |
| Dresses | one versatile dress that works with flats or boots, if the user wears dresses | 0–2 |
| Layers | a blazer, a trench | 2 |
| Coats | one warm coat in a base neutral | 1 |
| Shoes | white or leather trainers, loafers or ballet flats, ankle boots, one dressier pair | 3–5 |
| Bags | an everyday bag that fits their essentials, a small evening or crossbody bag | 1–3 |
| Accessories | belt, scarf, simple jewellery | as wanted |

## Proportions and mix-and-match test

- Aim for roughly two tops or knits per bottom.
- Each bottom should work with most tops; each top with most bottoms. A piece that only works with one other piece is an "orphan".
- Quick test: pick any top, bottom and layer at random. If most random combinations look fine, the capsule works.
- Mix silhouettes deliberately: a fitted top with a wide trouser, a looser knit with a straight jean or a slim skirt.

## Signs a wardrobe needs work

- "Nothing to wear" with a full closet → too many orphans or statement pieces, not enough core.
- Wearing the same three things → those are the true favourites; buy more of what makes them work and less of the rest.
- Lots of pieces with tags still on → buying for an imagined life rather than the real one. Revisit the "Life" questions.
